Sustainable Impact Isn't Built by Squeezing Profit from People, It's Built on Human Flourishing

I spent a decade in tech organizations that prioritized profit over people, and I burnt out. I thought something was wrong with me. I got my Masters in Management Science at Stanford to answer two questions:

How did we build work cultures that feel this bad, and what does it actually take to make work a place where we can flourish?

When our workplaces starve us of purpose, safety, and connection, productivity collapses and missions fail. Unfortunately, a lot of strategic advice focuses entirely on the organization's visible top layer: the programs on the website, organizational values posted in the breakroom, and public metrics in marketing materials. But lasting impact is determined by what's under the surface in the organization's understory: the daily decisions, relational trust, and hidden workflows that support the entire ecosystem.

The theory of change behind Understory is built on empowering each organization's understory, because that's what truly makes or breaks operational sustainability. When an organization deliberately designs its systems around human flourishing, it cultivates intrinsic motivation and sustains long-term impact.

Beneath the Surface: Breaking the Vicious Cycle

Nobody wakes up hell-bent on designing a soul-crushing workplace. (Well, most people don't.) Good people get conditioned by systemic constraints:

  • Founders are conditioned to create business models that prioritize survival and balance sheets over mission.
  • Business leaders are conditioned to make decisions that reinforce the business model.
  • Employees are conditioned to believe they can have profit or purpose, but not both.

Under these conditions, most employees burn out, disengage, or quit. And when employee productivity stalls, growth and mission impact stalls, and the organization doubles down on profit to "do more with less" so it can survive. It's a vicious, exhausting cycle.

But we can cultivate a different ecosystem. Many companies use this cycle for good:

  • Founders believe financial sustainability and mission impact can coexist, creating a business model that embodies both.
  • Business leaders believe their daily decisions can support their org's mission.
  • Employees believe their daily work can directly support their org's mission, too.

In this version of the cycle, employees gain motivation and productivity increases, which leads to financial sustainability and mission impact. When your people flourish, the work takes care of itself. Motivation stops feeling like an uphill battle, because people actually want to be there.

Walking With You, Not Lecturing At You

Understory focuses on the leadership level of this cycle, working alongside you to translate your high-level mission into practical, daily reality. By helping you redesign your internal systems and remove operational friction, we cultivate the conditions your people need to connect everyday work to lasting impact.

Most consultants point out everything you're doing wrong and walk away before the hard part starts. I started Understory because I refuse to do that to leaders. Tending to an organization's internal ecosystem is messy, emotional work. It takes patience, vulnerability, and someone who has been in the fire to walk alongside you. Grounded in neuroscience and human-centered design, my work looks beyond quick fixes at three long-term levers:

  • Priorities: How might we proactively tackle root causes beneath the surface, rather than reactively treating symptoms?
  • Habits: How might we motivate your team to consistently make decisions that increase mission impact?
  • Systems: How might we redesign processes, tooling, and governance so doing work that supports the mission is natural and frictionless?

I don't leave you with a presentation. I stay with you until your team feels the weight lift, the friction dissolve, and the joy of purposeful work return.
